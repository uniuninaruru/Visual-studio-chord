# Open textbook register for the harmony and counterpoint engine

Checked 2026-09-25. This repository records chapter links and independently written implementation rules; it does not copy textbook chapters, scores, or audio. Historical style exercises are not universal prohibitions for jazz or modern music.

## Source 1: Open Music Theory, second edition

- Authors include Mark Gotham, Kyle Gullings, Chelsey Hamm, Bryn Hughes, Brian Jarvis, Megan Lavengood, and John Peterson. Its [introduction and license](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/front-matter/introduction/) specify **CC BY-SA 4.0**, except where otherwise noted. Check individual credits before reusing quotations or media.
- [First species](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/first-species-counterpoint/): consonance, line independence, perfect parallels, openings and endings (`CP-1`).
- [Second species](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/second-species-counterpoint/): strong-beat consonance and passing dissonance on weak beats (`CP-2`).
- [Third species](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/third-species-counterpoint/): passing and neighboring relationships at shorter values (`CP-3`).
- [Fourth species](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/fourth-species-counterpoint/): preparation, suspension and resolution (`CP-4`).
- [Harmony and cadence](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/intro-to-harmony/): plan functional harmony around phrase endings (`H-FORM`).
- [Jazz voicings](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/jazz-voicings/): third/seventh connections, low-register spacing, optional fifth (`J-GUIDE`).
- [Chord-scale theory](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/chord-scale-theory/): chord tones on accents and embellishment; scales alone do not determine voice leading (`J-MELODY`).
- [Blues harmony](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/blues-harmony/): do not classify blues I7 and IV7 as unresolved ordinary V7 chords (`J-BLUES`).

## Source 2: Music Theory for the 21st-Century Classroom

- Robert Hutchinson. The [book site](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/) offers the online text and September 2025 PDF. Its [colophon](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/colophon-1.html) grants the **GNU Free Documentation License 1.2 or later**, without invariant sections or cover texts. Reprinting the text requires compliance with that license; this register only links and independently summarizes.
- [Voice Leading](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/VoiceLeading.html): independent, singable lines (`VL-INDEPENDENCE`).
- [Objectionable Parallels](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/ObjectionableParallels.html): strict-profile fifth and octave checks (`CP-PARALLEL`).
- [Jazz Chord Voicings](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/JazzChordVoicings.html): guide tones, shells and extensions (`J-VOICING`).
- [Standard Chord Progressions](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/StandardChordProgressions.html): ii–V–I and related cadential plans (`J-CADENCE`).

## Adoption sequence

1. First species and voicing: ranges, crossing, sounding consonance and guide-tone continuity on a shared time grid.
2. Second/third species and embellishment: settle accent targets first; add second-species passing, third-species neighboring and jazz chromatic approaches, then verify adjacent notes.
3. Fourth species and phrase harmony: represent a suspension as preparation, held dissonance and resolution; evaluate cadences, modulation and section seams across phrases.
4. Jazz chapters: apply third/seventh voice leading and style-specific extensions. Do not impose strict common-practice rules on blues or modal textures.

For each later source, add author, edition/update date, chapter URL, license, rule ID, applicable style and a contrast test. Engine coefficients are authored design parameters, not measured textbook statistics.
